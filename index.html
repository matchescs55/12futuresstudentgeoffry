Why it matters

We have been lucky before

Humans built nuclear weapons first and did the maths on the risks later. Four times, a small thing stood between us and disaster.

  1. 1961

    Goldsboro, North Carolina

    A bomber broke apart in the air and dropped two hydrogen bombs. One had four safety mechanisms. Three failed. A single switch was left.

  2. 1962

    Cuban Missile Crisis

    A Soviet submarine was under attack and out of contact. Launching a nuclear torpedo needed three officers to agree. Two said yes. Vasili Arkhipov said no.

  3. 1966

    Palomares, Spain

    A US bomber collided with a tanker plane and four hydrogen bombs fell. Two broke open on land and spread plutonium. One fell into the sea and took almost three months to find.

  4. 1983

    A bunker near Moscow

    The alarm said five US missiles were coming. Stanislav Petrov thought a real attack would not use only five, so he did not report it. It was sunlight reflecting on clouds.

With AI, first we build, then we count the risks.

My view

What I think

Nobody knows

Not the CEOs, not the researchers, not me. Anyone who says they know which future will happen is selling something.

Ask better questions

Instead of guessing the ending, I ask: who decides, and who can check? Control and power matter in almost every one of the twelve futures.

Use it, but check it

I use AI every day for study and work, and I will keep doing it. I also verify what it tells me and I do not accept fear or hype without evidence.
